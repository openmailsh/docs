When an email bounces, a recipient marks your email as spam, or someone unsubscribes, we automatically add them to your suppression list. Subsequent send attempts to that address are rejected.

## Suppression types

| Type | Trigger | What it means |
|------|---------|---------------|
| `bounce` | Hard bounce from recipient server | Address is invalid or doesn't exist |
| `complaint` | Recipient clicked "Report spam" | Recipient doesn't want email from you |
| `unsub` | Recipient unsubscribed | Recipient opted out |

## What happens when you send to a suppressed address

The send request returns `422` with a typed error:

```json
{
  "error": "recipient_suppressed",
  "type": "complaint"
}
```

Your agent should handle this gracefully - for example, by noting that the contact is unreachable and trying an alternative communication channel.

## One-click unsubscribe

Pass `"unsubscribe": true` on a [send](/api-reference/messages/send-email) to give the recipient a way to opt out. OpenMail adds `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post` headers (RFC 8058), so Gmail, Yahoo and Apple Mail show their own **Unsubscribe** button. Put `{{unsubscribe_url}}` in the body to show the same link in the message.

```json
{
  "to": "lead@example.com",
  "subject": "Quick question",
  "body": "Hi Dana, ...\n\nNot interested? Unsubscribe: {{unsubscribe_url}}",
  "unsubscribe": true
}
```

When the recipient unsubscribes, they become an `unsub` suppression for your whole organisation, not just the inbox that sent. You don't need to handle the opt-out yourself: later sends to them return `422 recipient_suppressed`.

The link opts out the `to` address only, so you can't combine `unsubscribe` with `cc`. Gmail and Yahoo require this header for bulk senders, and it's a simple way to give cold outreach the opt-out the law expects.

Suppressions are one part of deliverability. For domain setup, warm-up and content, see [Email deliverability](/best-practices/email-deliverability).
