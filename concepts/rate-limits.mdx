Two kinds of limit apply to every send:

- **Send limits** cap how fast an inbox sends, to anyone.
- **Cold outreach cap** limits how many **new recipients** you email per UTC day. Starts low and grows as you build history.

Replies and follow-ups only meet the first. Cold outreach meets both.

## Send limits

| Limit | Value | Scope |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Burst | 10/minute | Per inbox |
| Daily | 200/day | Per inbox |
| Inbox creation | 100/day | Per account |

Over one of these, the API returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header:

```json
{
  "error": "rate_limit_exceeded",
  "message": "Max 10 sends per minute per inbox"
}
```

## Cold outreach cap

A recipient is **new** the first time an inbox emails an address that has never written to it. Each To/Cc address counts once; Bcc, your own inboxes, anyone on one of your verified domains (see below), and anyone who has already emailed or been emailed by that inbox don't count. Warmth is per inbox, so a contact known to inbox A is still new to inbox B.

### Your own domains don't count

Mail to your own organisation isn't cold outreach, so addresses on your organisation's domains, and on their subdomains, never count as new. Three things make a domain yours:

- **A verified sending domain** covers its whole registrable domain. Verify `agents.acme.com` and `acme.com`, `hr.acme.com` and every other `*.acme.com` are covered. Only the registrable domain qualifies: `you.example-hosting.com` on a shared host doesn't cover the rest of that host.
- **A team member's login.** If someone on your team signs in with `name@acme.com`, `acme.com` is covered. Public mailbox providers such as `gmail.com` don't count.
- **A TXT record** for any other domain you own, such as a second brand your agents don't send from. Add it under [Exempt from the cold cap](https://console.openmail.sh/domains#exempt), publish the one record shown (`_openmail-owner.<domain>`), and verify. You can also use `POST /v1/owned-domains`. Up to 20 per organisation.

[Exempt from the cold cap](https://console.openmail.sh/domains#exempt) lists every covered domain. Domains covered by a sending domain or a login show as **Inherited**. Open a TXT-verified domain to see its DNS record and the mail your inboxes sent to it. `GET /v1/limits/cold` returns the same list as `internalDomains`.

Coverage is re-checked. A sending domain that fails DNS, or a TXT record that disappears, loses the exemption until you fix it.

## Check your limits

`GET /v1/limits/cold` returns today's caps before you hit them: the account cap and what you've used, every verified domain's tier and what unlocks the next one, and a `nextStep` sentence your agent can act on. Nothing is counted or changed by calling it.

```json
{
  "mode": "reputation",
  "resetsAt": "2026-09-30T00:00:00.000Z",
  "reputationState": "good",
  "account": {
    "limit": 150,
    "usedToday": 37,
    "remaining": 113,
    "ceiling": 500,
    "avgNewPerDay7d": 31.4,
    "replyRate": 0.168,
    "sustainPerDayToGrow": 76
  },
  "domains": [
    {
      "domain": "mail.acme.com",
      "tier": 2,
      "limit": 100,
      "usedToday": 17,
      "nextTier": { "limit": null, "minAgeDays": 28, "minLifetimeFirstContacts": 400, "daysToGo": 0, "contactsToGo": 61 },
      "health": { "state": "good", "volume7d": 1240, "bounceRate7d": 0.008, "complaintRate7d": 0 }
    }
  ],
  "nextStep": "You've emailed 37 of 150 new recipients today. Your 7-day average is 31.4/day; sustain more than 76/day and the cap rises (up to 500/day). ..."
}
```

`sustainPerDayToGrow` is the number to plan around. Average more than that many new recipients a day over a week, keep bounces and complaints clean, and the account cap rises. A `nextTier.limit` of `null` means the domain graduates to the account cap.

Each domain's `health` rolls up its inboxes over the last 7 days. `state` is the worst inbox's [reputation](#reputation); the rates are weighted by volume.

An inbox-scoped key sees its own inbox and domain. Pass `?inboxId=` with an account key to get the same view for one inbox. The API reference lists every field.

[Settings → Limits](https://console.openmail.sh/settings/limits) shows the same numbers. Three cards cover new people today, exempt domains and growth. Below them, a table lists every sending domain with its tier, today's use and health. The console numbers tiers 1 to 4; the API's `tier` is 0 to 3.

## When you get a 429

```json
{
  "error": "cold_outreach_limit",
  "message": "This sending domain has reached today's limit of new recipients. The limit rises as the domain ages and builds a clean sending history. Try again after midnight UTC.",
  "scope": "domain",
  "limit": 20,
  "resetsAt": "2026-09-30T00:00:00.000Z",
  "nextStep": "This domain is on a warm-up tier of 20 new recipients/day. It moves to 50/day once it is 7+ days old and has reached 50+ first contacts. Check GET /v1/limits/cold for where it stands. Mail to acme.com counted as new. If that is your organisation's domain, verify it at https://console.openmail.sh/domains and mail to it stops counting."
}
```

<Steps>
  <Step title="Read the scope">
    `inbox`, `domain` or `customer` (your account). It tells you which cap you hit, so you know whether another inbox or domain would help. On a `domain` block, another inbox on the same domain won't.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pause cold sends until resetsAt">
    Caps reset at midnight UTC. Retrying earlier fails the same way. Call `GET /v1/limits/cold` to see what's left on your other inboxes and domains, and `nextStep` for what raises this cap.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep replying">
    The rejected send didn't go out and didn't count. Replies and follow-ups to existing contacts are unaffected, so your agents can keep working threads.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Reputation

Every inbox has a reputation state: `good`, `watch`, `throttled` or `suspended`. It's computed from the inbox's permanent bounces and spam complaints over the trailing 7 days. Keep bounces under 5% and complaints under 0.1% and the inbox stays in `good`, with full caps.

### Thresholds

| Signal | `watch` | `throttled` | `suspended` |
|--------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Bounce rate | Any bounce | 5% | 10% |
| Complaint rate | Any complaint | 0.1% | 0.3% |

### What each state does

| State | Effect | How it clears |
|-------|--------|---------------|
| `watch` | Caps stop growing; a custom domain drops one tier | On its own, as the rate falls |
| `throttled` | Daily send limit cut to 25%; cold caps halved | On its own, as the rate falls |
| `suspended` | Every send from the inbox refused | After review by OpenMail; the `inbox.reactivated` [webhook](/pages/webhooks/events) fires |

### If an inbox is suspended

1. Fix what caused the bounces or complaints: validate the list, check DNS, drop the source.
2. Contact us for review.
3. Wait for the `inbox.reactivated` webhook before sending again.

For how to send so you never meet these limits, read [Email deliverability](/best-practices/email-deliverability).
