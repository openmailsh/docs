## Send limits

Fixed limits on how fast an inbox can send and how many inboxes an account can create. They don't change with plan or history.

| Limit | Value | Scope |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Burst | 10/minute | Per inbox |
| Daily | 200/day | Per inbox |
| Inbox creation | 100/day | Per account |

Over one of these, the API returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header:

```json
{
  "error": "rate_limit_exceeded",
  "message": "Max 10 sends per minute per inbox"
}
```

## Cold outreach cap

The cold outreach cap limits how many **new recipients** your agents can email per UTC day. Providers judge a sender by how many strangers it writes to and how those strangers react, so this is the cap that protects your domain's reputation.

### What counts as a new recipient

A recipient is new on the first email **from an inbox** to an address that has never written to that inbox. Each unique To/Cc address on a send counts once. Warmth is per inbox: a contact who wrote to inbox A is still new for inbox B.

### What doesn't count

- Replies to anyone who has emailed the inbox.
- Follow-ups to someone the inbox has already emailed.
- Bcc addresses. They're billed, not metered as cold.
- Your own inboxes on the same account, so a deliverability probe between your mailboxes costs nothing.

    | 0 | 0+ | 0+ | 20 |

    | 2 | 14+ | 150+ | 100 |
    | 3 | 28+ | 400+ | your account cap |

    Age is counted on OpenMail, from the day the domain verified; how long you've owned the domain doesn't matter, since providers only start scoring it when it sends through us. The volume condition stops a domain that sat idle for a month from jumping straight to the account cap, because providers have never seen it send. A proven account that adds a fresh domain still starts that domain at tier 0; providers score the domain, not your account. Inboxes on the shared `omail.sh` domain have no domain cap.

    **Account.** 150/day across all inboxes during warm-up. Afterwards, 2× your account's trailing 7-day average of new recipients per day, min 150, max 500. The account cap is the ceiling on everything: adding domains or inboxes never pushes you past it.

    **Account warm-up** ends after 7 days or 100 lifetime new recipients, whichever comes first.

    **Growth** of the account cap is gated by engagement once you have enough history. With 200 or more cold threads opened in the 14 days ending 3 days ago, the daily multiplier depends on how many got a human reply: 1% or more gives 2×, 0.5% or more gives 1.5×, below that the cap holds at your average. Below 200 threads the 2× applies.

    **Reputation state** only ever lowers a cap. `watch` holds the account cap at your trailing average and drops a domain one tier; `throttled` halves every cap; `suspended` sets them to 0. See [Reputation](#reputation).

### When you hit the cap

The API returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header and names the scope that blocked the send:

```json
{
  "error": "cold_outreach_limit",
  "message": "This sending domain has reached today's limit of new recipients. The limit rises as the domain ages and builds a clean sending history. Try again after midnight UTC.",
  "scope": "domain",
  "limit": 20,
  "resetsAt": "2026-09-30T00:00:00.000Z"
}
```

`scope` is `inbox`, `domain` or `customer` (the account). The send is rejected whole: no recipient on it is counted and nothing goes out. Replies and follow-ups keep going.

There's no endpoint to read today's remaining allowance yet, so schedulers should treat the `429` as the signal and back off until `resetsAt`.

## Reputation

Every inbox and pod has a reputation state: `good`, `watch`, `throttled` or `suspended`. It's computed from permanent bounces and spam complaints once an inbox is at least 3 days old or has sent 20 emails. Rates are judged over the trailing 7 days, plus a 24-hour burst check that catches a fast spike at lower volume.

| Signal | `watch` | `throttled` | `suspended` |
|--------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Bounces (≥ 100 sends in 7d) | Any bounce | Rate ≥ 5% | Rate ≥ 10% and ≥ 5 bounces |
| Bounce burst (≥ 20 sends in 24h) | | | Rate ≥ 10% and ≥ 5 bounces |
| Complaints (≥ 1,000 sends in 7d) | Any complaint | Rate ≥ 0.1% | Rate ≥ 0.3% and ≥ 3 complaints |
| Complaint burst (≥ 200 sends in 24h) | | | Rate ≥ 0.3% and ≥ 3 complaints |

<Warning>
  30 probe sends in a day with 5 bounces among them is enough to suspend an inbox. Bounces to your own inboxes caused by a DNS problem count like any other bounce, so fix the DNS before probing.
</Warning>

`throttled` cuts the inbox's daily send limit to 25% and halves its cold cap. `suspended` blocks every send from the inbox; the API refuses with a message saying the inbox is suspended for reputation. `watch` and `throttled` recover on their own as the rate falls. A suspension does not: OpenMail lifts it after review, and the `inbox.reactivated` [webhook](/pages/webhooks/events) fires when that happens.

For how to send so you never meet these limits, read [Email deliverability](/best-practices/email-deliverability).
