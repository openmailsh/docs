OpenMail enforces rate limits to protect domain reputation and ensure deliverability for all customers.

## Send limits

| Limit | Value | Scope |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Burst | 10/minute | Per inbox |
| Daily | 200/day | Per inbox |
| Inbox creation | 100/day | Per account |

## Cold outreach cap

The cold outreach cap limits how many **new recipients** your agents can email per UTC day. It is the reputation guard: providers judge a sender by how many strangers it writes to and how they react, not by raw volume.

### What counts as a new recipient

A recipient is new on the first email **from an inbox** to an address that has never written to that inbox. Each unique To/Cc address on a send counts once. These are **not** counted:

- Replies to anyone who has emailed the inbox ("warm" recipients).
- Follow-ups to someone the inbox has already emailed.
- Bcc addresses (they are billed, but not metered as cold).
- Your own inboxes on the same account, so deliverability probes between your mailboxes cost nothing.

Warmth is per inbox: a contact who wrote to inbox A is still new for inbox B.

### Pro: reputation-driven cap

On Pro the cap is not a fixed number. It starts at a floor and grows with clean sending history. Three scopes are checked, in this order; the first one you exceed produces the `429`.

| Scope | During warm-up | After warm-up |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| **Inbox** | 20/day per inbox | No per-inbox cold cap (the 200/day send limit still applies) |
| **Sending domain** (custom domains only) | 20/day across all inboxes on that domain | 2× the domain's trailing 7-day average of new recipients per day, min 20, max your account cap |
| **Account** | 150/day across all inboxes | 2× your account's trailing 7-day average, min 150, max 500 |

**Warm-up** ends after **7 days or 100 lifetime new recipients**, whichever comes first. It is tracked separately for the account (from account creation) and for each custom domain (from the day it verified). A proven account that adds a fresh domain still warms that domain up from 20/day, because providers score the domain, not your account.

Every verified domain gets its own warm-up allowance, so N fresh domains can send up to N × 20 new recipients per day. The account cap is the ceiling on the total: adding domains or inboxes never pushes you past it. Inboxes on the shared `omail.sh` domain have no domain cap.

**Growth** is gated by engagement once you have enough history. With 200 or more cold threads opened in the 14 days ending 3 days ago, the daily multiplier depends on how many got a human reply: 1% or more → 2×, 0.5% or more → 1.5×, below that the cap holds at your average. Below 200 threads the 2× applies.

**Reputation state** scales the cap as well: `watch` holds it at your trailing average, `throttled` halves it, `suspended` sets it to 0. See [Reputation](#reputation) below.

### Free: fixed caps

Free sends from the shared domain, so its reputation is everyone's. The caps are fixed and age-based:

| Scope | First 30 days | After 30 days |
|-------|---------------|---------------|
| **Inbox** | 20/day | 200/day |
| **Account** | 30/day | 100/day |

### Enterprise

Enterprise accounts have negotiated caps. Contact us to set them.

### Rate limit response

When a cold cap is hit the API returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header and tells you which scope blocked the send:

```json
{
  "error": "cold_outreach_limit",
  "message": "You've reached today's limit of new recipients. Try again after midnight UTC.",
  "scope": "domain",
  "limit": 20,
  "resetsAt": "2026-09-30T00:00:00.000Z"
}
```

`scope` is `inbox`, `domain` or `customer` (the account). The send is rejected whole: no recipient on it is counted, and nothing is sent. Replies and follow-ups keep going.

There is no endpoint to read today's remaining allowance yet. Schedulers should treat the `429` as the signal and back off until `resetsAt`.

### Raising the cap

On Pro the cap grows on its own: send steadily, keep bounces and complaints low, and get replies. If you have a vetted use case that needs volume before the history exists, contact us; we can set a trusted volume that replaces the warm-up.

## Reputation

Every inbox and pod has a reputation state: `good`, `watch`, `throttled` or `suspended`. It is computed from permanent bounces and spam complaints, once an inbox is at least 3 days old or has sent 20 emails. Rates are judged over the trailing 7 days, plus a 24-hour burst check that catches a fast spike at lower volume.

| Signal | `watch` | `throttled` | `suspended` |
|--------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Bounces (≥ 100 sends in 7d) | Any bounce | Rate ≥ 5% | Rate ≥ 10% and ≥ 5 bounces |
| Bounce burst (≥ 20 sends in 24h) | | | Rate ≥ 10% and ≥ 5 bounces |
| Complaints (≥ 1,000 sends in 7d) | Any complaint | Rate ≥ 0.1% | Rate ≥ 0.3% and ≥ 3 complaints |
| Complaint burst (≥ 200 sends in 24h) | | | Rate ≥ 0.3% and ≥ 3 complaints |

So 30 probe sends in a day with 5 bounces among them is enough to suspend an inbox. Bounces to your own inboxes caused by a DNS problem count like any other bounce: fix the DNS before probing.

`throttled` cuts the inbox's daily send limit to 25% and halves its cold cap. `suspended` blocks sending from the inbox with `403 reputation_suspended`. `watch` and `throttled` recover on their own as the rate falls. A suspension does not: OpenMail lifts it after review, and the `inbox.reactivated` [webhook](/pages/webhooks/events) fires when that happens.

## Other limits

When a send limit is hit, the API returns `429` with a `Retry-After` header:

```json
{
  "error": "rate_limit_exceeded",
  "message": "Max 10 sends per minute per inbox"
}
```

## Why this matters

Fresh domains on shared IP pools need time to build reputation. If your agents send too much cold outreach too quickly, emails will land in spam - which hurts your agents' effectiveness and your domain's reputation. These limits exist to protect deliverability.

Contact us if you need higher limits or a dedicated sending IP.
