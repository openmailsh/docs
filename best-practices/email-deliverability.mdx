This page assumes you know what SPF, DKIM and warm-up are. It covers what's different on OpenMail: the cold cap does the warm-up, and reputation is tracked per inbox and per domain.

## Domain

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add a custom domain">
    Reputation built on the shared `omail.sh` isn't yours. Add a [custom domain](/concepts/custom-domains) (Pro and above) and run outreach from there.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish DMARC too">
    The domain verifies without it, so it's easy to skip. Gmail and Yahoo require it from bulk senders. Keep an existing `_dmarc` record if you have one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Probe before you write to anyone">
    Send between two inboxes on the new domain. It doesn't count against the cold cap, and a bounce here means a DNS problem.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Keep cold mail on its own subdomain, like `outreach.yourcompany.com`, so a reputation hit never touches the domain your team uses. Several products or clients: one domain each. Each domain ramps on its own, but the account cap still applies, so this buys isolation, not volume.
</Tip>

## Sending

Warm-up is built in. A new domain ramps as it ages and as you actually reach people; there's no schedule to follow and no need to hit the cap every day.

| Days since domain added | New recipients messaged so far | New recipients/day |
|------------------------:|-------------------------------:|-------------------:|
| 0+ | 0+ | 20 |
| 7+ | 50+ | 50 |
| 14+ | 150+ | 100 |
| 28+ | 400+ | your account cap |

Three things that slow it down:

- **Adding inboxes for volume.** Caps are per domain and per account, so 50 inboxes on a new domain still share 20 new recipients a day. Create inboxes for separate identities, not throughput.
- **Bursting.** 10 a minute per inbox is the hard limit, but a domain that sends 50 at 09:00 and nothing until tomorrow looks like a blast. Queue cold sends over the working day.
- **Retrying a 429.** The response names the cap and `resetsAt` (midnight UTC). Another inbox on the same domain hits the same cap. See [When you get a 429](/concepts/rate-limits#when-you-get-a-429).

<Warning>
  Over 5% bounces in a week throttles the inbox; 10% suspends it, and only a review lifts that. Validate addresses; don't buy lists.
</Warning>

## The first message

Filters read the first message to a stranger more harshly than anything after it, and a reply is what grows your cap. Write to earn one.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <div className="card-do">
    <Card title="Do" icon="check">
      - Open with their name and something specific to them
      - Ask one question; leave the pitch for the reply
      - Keep it short enough to read on a phone without scrolling
      - Send the link once they've answered
    </Card>
  </div>
  <div className="card-dont">
    <Card title="Don't" icon="x">
      - Open with a sentence that could have gone to anyone
      - Use "free", "limited time", capitals or exclamation marks
      - Put links or images in the first message
      - Add a tracking pixel — it's an image, and trips the same filters
    </Card>
  </div>
</Columns>

Plain text is handled: the API generates a text part for every HTML message and adds no tracking pixel.

## Bounces and complaints

An address that bounces, complains or unsubscribes goes on your [suppression list](/concepts/suppressions) and later sends to it return `422 recipient_suppressed`. When your agent sees that, drop the contact or try another channel. Don't look for a way around it; a second bounce to the same address is what costs reputation.
